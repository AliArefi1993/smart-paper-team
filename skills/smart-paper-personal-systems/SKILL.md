# Personal systems review

Use this skill only to advise Product and Designer. Read `STATUS.md`, then narrow the task to the relevant `PRODUCT.md` behavior, design record, user feedback, and implemented flow. The Android local-data app is the primary product. Do not treat an unshipped design or backend-only behavior as Android behavior.

Look at the person's complete loop: capture an intention or thought, choose what matters, plan time, start and stop focused work, record or review money, and reflect. For the affected steps, ask:

- What must the person remember, re-enter, or switch screens to do? Does the app make the next action and saved state clear?
- Can the person use each area independently, skip a practice, change plans, and recover after an interruption without losing work or feeling punished?
- Does a proposed connection reduce effort while respecting privacy, especially for finance and personal notes? Avoid silent sharing or implying that timer activity measures productivity.
- Will it work with realistic long content, short phone sessions, English and Persian, and differing weekly rhythms?

Use domain judgment carefully: planning benefits from visible priorities and flexible rescheduling; focus sessions from clear start, pause, resume, and stopping choices; money tracking from understandable records and privacy; notes and ideas from low-friction capture and later rediscovery. These are lenses for evaluating a proposal, not claims about every user or instructions to add features.

Report only findings and candidate ideas. For each worthwhile idea, state the observed friction or explicit user request, the smallest useful change to consider, the likely benefit, and a cost, risk, or question to test. Mark unsupported assumptions `Needs confirmation`. Give Product problem and success-signal input; give Designer workflow and state considerations. Do not set priorities or acceptance criteria, prescribe visual layout, edit files, create tickets, run tests, or implement anything.
